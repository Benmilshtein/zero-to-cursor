Cursor calls itself "the AI-first code editor." That's a marketing line. What does it actually mean in practice?

Most editors *let you bolt AI onto them* with a plugin — GitHub Copilot in VS Code, for example. The AI lives in a corner. It autocompletes. Useful, but separate.

Cursor flips the relationship. AI is built into the **core interactions** of the editor:

1. **Tab** — predictive autocomplete that often suggests entire blocks, not just the next word.
2. **Cmd+K (Ctrl+K on Windows/Linux)** — highlight code, press a key, describe a change in plain English, watch it apply.
3. **Cmd+L** — a chat panel that knows about your codebase.
4. **Composer / Agent** — describe a feature; Cursor edits multiple files, runs commands, and reports back.

The point isn't "AI in the editor." The point is that **every action you take has an AI version available**. You can still type code by hand. But the moment you want help, it's one keystroke away — and it's already looking at your project.

That's the difference. Not magic. Just deeper integration.
